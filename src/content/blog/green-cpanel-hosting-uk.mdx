Search for green web hosting and you'll mostly find the modern platforms — Cloudflare Pages, Netlify, Vercel — which are excellent, verified, and [what we recommend for static sites](/blog/green-web-hosting-uk-compared). They also share a trait nobody mentions: there's no cPanel in sight.

That matters more than the modern-stack world admits. If you run WordPress, manage email at your own domain, hand sites over to clients who expect a familiar panel, or simply want one place for files, databases, and backups — you need traditional hosting. And the honest question is whether you can have that *and* verified renewable infrastructure. You can. The list is just short.

## Why the overlap is so thin

Traditional cPanel hosting is the old economy of the web: shared servers, long-lived accounts, thin margins. Sustainability investment went first to the hyperscale platforms, whose scale made renewable contracts easy to sign and easy to publicise. The result is a strange gap — the most common kind of small-business hosting in Britain is the kind least likely to advertise its energy source. The providers below are the exceptions, all listed in [The Green Web Foundation's](https://www.thegreenwebfoundation.org/green-web-check/) verified directory.

## What to check before you move

1. **The directory, not the badge.** A leaf logo is design; a [Green Web Foundation listing](https://www.thegreenwebfoundation.org/green-web-check/) is evidence. Check the provider — and after migrating, check your own domain shows as verified.
2. **Green at the base tier.** Some hosts sell "eco" as a premium variant of the same racks. If the infrastructure is renewable, it's renewable for the £3 plan too; don't pay twice for the same electrons.
3. **Where the data centre actually is.** UK data residency matters for some clients and contracts — Krystal and Kualo keep data in the UK; several well-known "green" names don't.
4. **The email plan.** Mailboxes are where cPanel migrations go wrong. Move them first, switch MX records last, and keep the old host live until every mailbox is confirmed. (Or have someone do it for you — this is most of what "managed" means in practice.)
5. **Your page weight.** Hosting is the second-biggest lever, not the first. [Our audit of 100 UK small-business sites](/blog/we-audited-100-uk-small-business-websites) found plenty of heavy pages sitting proudly on green infrastructure. [Run your site through the free carbon audit](/website-carbon-audit) before you migrate — if the page itself is the problem, fix that too and the migration finishes the job properly.
