The honest answer to "how much CO₂ does a website produce?" is *it depends on two numbers* — how heavy each page view is, and how many views you get. But "it depends" is a cop-out without data, so here are the actual figures from [our July 2026 audit of 100 UK small-business homepages](/blog/we-audited-100-uk-small-business-websites), measured with the same Sustainable Web Design Model used by most carbon tools.

## Per page view: the honest range

- **The median UK small-business homepage: 0.24 g of CO₂e per view** — a D on the A+ to F scale, transferring 1.8 MB of data.
- **The global median page is around 0.36 g** (an E). Half the web does better, half worse.
- **A well-built page: under 0.08 g** (an A) — and under 0.04 g is A+ territory, the top 5% worldwide. Eighteen of our 99 sites managed it, so this is not an elite standard.
- **The extremes in one national sample:** 0.003 g to 2.8 g per view. The heaviest homepage emitted nearly a thousand times more than the lightest, per visit, for the same job — being a small business's front door.

## Per year: multiply by your traffic

Grams per view only mean something once traffic is applied. Using the study's median (0.24 g):

| Monthly views | CO₂e per year (median page) | CO₂e per year (heavy page, 1 g/view) |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | ~3 kg | ~12 kg |
| 10,000 | ~29 kg | ~120 kg |
| 100,000 | ~290 kg | ~1.2 tonnes |

For scale: 29 kg is roughly 65 miles in a petrol car; 1.2 tonnes is in the territory of a transatlantic flight seat. A single popular page on a heavy site can quietly out-emit a business's entire paper use — and unlike most footprint lines, this one is invisible until someone measures it.

Two things follow from the table. First, most small-business websites are a minor line in the company footprint — worth fixing, not worth panicking over. Second, the *ratio* is the scandal: the heavy column is the same business reaching the same customers, just with an unconsidered web page. This is the cheapest emissions reduction most companies will ever find, because [the fix also makes the site faster](/blog/sustainable-web-design-practical-guide).

## What actually drives the number

From the study's byte-by-byte breakdown of everything those 100 pages transferred:

- **Images: 60.5% of all bytes.** Not video, not JavaScript — photography, uploaded at camera size. 44 of 99 sites shipped over 1 MB of images on the homepage alone.
- **Video and media: 16%** — mostly autoplaying backgrounds nobody asked for.
- **JavaScript: 10%** — the villain of most performance writing turns out to be a supporting actor on small-business sites.
- **Fonts: 10%** — a quiet one; 43 sites shipped over 200 KB of web fonts, most of which never render a visible character.
- **Hosting** doesn't change the bytes but changes the emissions per byte: verified renewable infrastructure meaningfully cuts the per-view figure — though [not by enough to rescue a heavy page](/blog/carbon-neutral-vs-green-web-hosting).
